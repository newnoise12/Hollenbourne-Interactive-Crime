# Hollenbourne — Action Economy Reference

*Everything designed across this project, pulled into one place. Individual student budgets, not team budgets — each student gets their own weekly points, spent on whatever they choose, no approval needed from teammates. The team-shared resource is the evidence pool and case reserve, not the points themselves.*

## The budget mechanic

- **Baseline: 1 action point per student, per week.**
- **Trust bonus: 0–3, calculated from that week's individual quiz score(s), averaged across the team, applied identically to every member.** Resets weekly — does not carry over.
- **Banking (the case reserve):** a student may convert 2 unspent points into 1 reserve point, added to a team-wide, permanently accumulating pool. A student with only the baseline (1 point, no trust bonus that week) cannot bank — banking requires having earned a bonus, and this now falls out of the numbers rather than needing a special rule.
- **The reserve is spendable by anyone on the team, at any time from when it exists onward** — not restricted to a specific week, though week 11 is likely where teams choose to use it given the stakes. Spending it should trigger a confirmation prompt ("this will use your team's shared reserve — do you have your team's agreement?") rather than requiring actual approval.
- **Reserve cap:** to be decided once real play-testing shows whether one's needed. Leaning toward a fairly high cap if any — deliberately allows a comeback for a team that's fallen behind in earlier weeks.

### Tool-mark chain

| Action | Cost | Gated behind |
|---|---|---|
| Comparative forensic review — Mason and Wooley wound patterns | 3 | — |
| Search a named suspect's property (home and workplace) | 2 each | Comparative review above |
| Interim interview — Martin Burgess, voluntary under caution | 1 | Toolkit search above, naming Burgess; **and** pull initial Burgess interview |

**Deliberately renamed from "search a named suspect's toolkit."** The old name told students what they were looking for before they'd looked — a search framed around finding a *toolkit* specifically all but announces the outcome. "Property search" is the neutral, procedural framing a real request would actually carry, and it applies identically across all three suspects below rather than being Burgess-specific language wearing a thin disguise.

**This is a genuine three-way search, not a single check that happens to be "about" Burgess.** Each of the three original suspects can be searched separately, at 2 points each, and each turns up something that's *plausibly* the weapon on first look — the point is comparison, not confirmation-on-demand:

- **Martin Burgess** — his work van and flat. The van holds his gas-engineer's kit; one spanner is conspicuously cleaner than the rest of the set. Forensic comparison: **matches** the tool-mark signature (flat, parallel impact surfaces).
- **Nigel Wooley** — his flat. A steel surveyor's levelling pole, kept in a hall cupboard, left over from his old career. Genuinely heavy, genuinely could cause blunt trauma. Forensic comparison: **does not match** — smooth cylindrical cross-section, inconsistent with the flat parallel-surface signature. The same cupboard also holds an ordinary household toolkit — hammer, screwdrivers, and an adjustable spanner, the kind of thing most homes have. The spanner is the same *type* of tool as the murder weapon, so it can't be ruled out by shape — it's ruled out on **wear pattern** instead: ordinary, infrequent DIY use, no sign of the unusual cleaning that flagged Burgess's. A genuinely tenser moment than the pole, and a different forensic reasoning skill: condition, not shape.
- **Colin Swayne** — his flat. An old claw hammer, unclear origin, kept in a kitchen drawer — the kind of thing that accumulates in most homes without anyone remembering where it came from. Forensic comparison: **does not match** — the claw and rounded head don't produce the established wound pattern.

If reached by naming Burgess specifically, the search resolves via a search warrant (PACE s.8) executed on both the van and the flat — reasonable grounds exist (the tool-mark match, his trade, his 2019 interview) without yet meeting the threshold to arrest. This is a distinct legal basis from the endgame's search, which happens as part of the arrest itself (PACE s.32) — worth keeping the two clearly separate so nothing contradicts Appendix H. The interim interview (Appendix L) is voluntary but under caution, reachable for just 6 points total, and ends inconclusively — no arrest, no admission, a genuine judgement call for a team on whether his composure reads as clean or practised. Searching Nigel or Swayne doesn't unlock an equivalent interview escalation — their objects are ruled out on forensic grounds alone, which is itself the point: a team that searches only Burgess never learns that finding *something plausible* isn't the same as finding *the* weapon.

## Rough supply check

A team of 5 students, over just the three concentrated weeks (9–11), generates a minimum of 15 points from baseline alone (5 students × 1 point × 3 weeks), before any trust bonus at all. With even a modest average bonus across those weeks, 25–30+ points across the team is realistic. Against a 12-point endgame cost, that leaves real room for the other threads (Swayne's Mason connection, Nigel's parallel route, the homeowner witness) without the endgame feeling unreachable — but this is a rough estimate, not a tested one, and worth revisiting once actual weekly quiz/trust content exists to check the bonus assumption against.
