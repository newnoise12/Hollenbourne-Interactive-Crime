*Every piece of content generated across the whole project, organised by when and how it should reach students. Four categories: never distributed, free from day one, free but time-released, and bought with trust points. A few items turned up with no clear existing gating — flagged at the end rather than silently assigned one.*

## Category 0: Never distributed to students

**The case bible's "The true solution" section** (who really killed whom, and why) is the answer key. It should never appear in any student-facing material, extract, or Claude Code prompt that touches the student-facing app. Worth being explicit about this since it's the single most important exclusion in the whole project — everything else on this list is safe to expose in some form or at some point; this one item never is.

- **Sara Butt's missing person report**
- **The town map and Boresfield street map** — pure orientation material, no reason to gate geography itself
- **The homicide rate background** — framing/podcast material

**Deliberately not on this list: the initial forensic reports.** These now sit in Category 3 instead — see below for why.

## Category 2: Free, but time-released rather than bought

Only one strong candidate turned up for this category, but it's a genuinely important one.

- **Sara Butt's follow-up forensic report** (HP-FS-2025-0203-S — the strangulation finding that supersedes "bludgeoned"). **Recommend releasing this in Week 10 or 11, not Week 9.** Handing it over on day one would flatten one of the case's best-built moments: Ferris's own memo argues "three of four deaths share blunt-force trauma" as a real pattern, and that argument is quietly undermined by a finding she didn't have yet. If students see the correction immediately, they never get to experience — and then have to reason their way through — the same pattern-matching trap Ferris fell into. Releasing it mid-way through the case-analysis weeks preserves that as a genuine "wait, that changes things" moment rather than a footnote. Once its week arrives, it carries the same small pull-cost as the other forensic reports below — time-gated *and* institutionally gatekept, not one or the other.

No other document earned a place in this category on review — everything else is either genuinely baseline or genuinely worth costing points, without an obvious reason to time-gate it independent of both.

**A note on why forensic reports specifically carry a cost, worth keeping consistent across everything built on this:** the premise is that the force is initially indifferent to the review panel at best, actively distrustful of outside consultants at worst. Policy logs, missing person reports, and maps are the kind of thing any liaison would hand over without a second thought. Pathology records are different — genuinely sensitive, chain-of-custody material a cautious or reluctant institution would want a proper formal request for, not something volunteered. That institutional friction is the whole justification for gating these at all, and it's worth reflecting in how the action is described to students (a mildly reluctant records office, a form to fill in) rather than just a bare cost number with no texture behind it.

## Flagged: no clear existing gating, needs a decision

Two documents turned up during this review with no costed action anywhere in the action economy pointing to them — meaning as things stand, they'd default to baseline/free, which may not be intentional:

- **Holly Creagan's witness statement** (the Swayne alibi) — this is meant to be the mechanism for the endgame's "wrong suspect" outcome on Porterhouse. If it's freely available from day one, a team could read it immediately rather than needing to think to pursue Swayne's alibi themselves — which weakens the "a team that never thinks to press further ends up wrong" design. **Recommend gating this behind a specific action** (something like "Request Holly Creagan's statement, re: Swayne's whereabouts," cost 1–2), rather than leaving it baseline.
- **Sara Butt's phone data extraction** (the "back in 10" WhatsApp timestamp) — less critical to gate, since it's mostly timeline-scaffolding rather than a discovery with narrative weight, but worth a deliberate decision either way rather than leaving it as an accidental default.

Both are quick fixes — a one-line addition to the action economy and unlock tree — but I didn't want to just assign them a cost silently without you weighing in, given one of them (Holly's statement) actually matters for how the endgame plays out.
