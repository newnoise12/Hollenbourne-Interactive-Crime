## Grading rubric

*Same scale for all four cases — 3 points each, 12 total — so no single case can dominate or sink the overall score just because it carries more narrative weight than the others.*

| Response | Points |
|---|---|
| Correct call, made confidently, evidence cited is specific and accurate | 3 |
| Correct call, but evidence cited is vague, thin, or only partly right | 1–2 |
| Wrong call, but hedged rather than confidently charged (e.g. "we suspect X but the evidence feels thin") | 0 |
| **Wrong call, confidently charged** | **−2** |

**What "correct" means per case, for your own reference while marking:**

- **Mason and Wooley:** Martin Burgess, with a genuine evidence chain behind it (not just a hunch).
- **Butt:** Martin Burgess. A team naming Nigel or Swayne here, confidently, is the clearest sign they didn't press far enough on the alibi threads (Nigel's cell site, Holly Creagan's statement).
- **Porterhouse:** the correct call is recognising it as a separate case and *not* charging Swayne or folding it into the pattern — Kato and Reece are deliberately unreachable through the evidence available, so naming them isn't expected or required. A team that writes something like "insufficient evidence to charge anyone, this looks like a different kind of case" should score the full 3, not be marked down for failing to name a suspect.

**The −2 row is deliberately the only negative score, and it's deliberately concentrated on Porterhouse in practice** — a team that confidently charges Swayne there is making the exact mistake the whole case is built to invite if you're not careful. The same penalty applies identically to any other confidently-wrong call (Nigel or Swayne for Butt, for instance), so it isn't a trap reserved for one case — it's a consistent rule that happens to matter most there.

**Total: out of 12**, added directly to the team's final points for the module.
