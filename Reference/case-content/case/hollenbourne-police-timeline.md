# Hollenbourne — Police Timeline of Investigative Actions

*Every forensic test, interview, and investigative step established across all four cases, in order. Pulled directly from the case bible — nothing new added here, just reorganised by action rather than by narrative. Actions that were *not* taken are marked as gaps, since those matter as much as the ones that were.*

---

## Case 1: Geoff Mason (found 8 October 2019)

| Date | Action | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 8 Oct 2019 | Body found, marsh | Investigation opened |
| 8 Oct 2019 (ongoing) | Forensic examination of body | Limited findings — evidence degraded by prolonged outdoor exposure |
| ~Oct 2019 | Neighbours interviewed | Little to say about "the quiet widower" who sometimes walked the marshland |
| ~Oct 2019 | Local residents' accounts taken re: general area activity | Confirm county lines presence in the area generally; nobody connects Mason to it specifically |
| **16 Oct 2019** | **Burgess interviewed voluntarily** (Appendix A) — declines solicitor | Gives detailed account; van telematics discrepancy (3:45 logged vs. 4:45 actual departure) challenged and explained away; DNA present but fully explained by legitimate boiler visit; ends with Burgess volunteering the county-lines explanation unprompted |
| — | **Gap: no vehicle check requested** | The dark 4x4 seen near the scene was never cross-referenced — Mason's case never generated enough investigative weight to justify it |
| — | **Gap: no search of Burgess's van/toolkit** | The murder weapon (a spanner, conspicuously cleaner than the rest of his set) was never found because nobody thought to ask which tool he'd used |
| Within ~1 week | Case deprioritised | Effectively cold from here |

## Case 2: Susan Wooley (found 6 January 2022)

| Date | Action | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 6 Jan 2022 | Body found, home | Investigation opened, led by DS Hannah Ferris |
| ~Jan 2022 | Forensic examination of property | DNA profile matching Nigel Wooley recovered (explained by prior cohabitation, undateable); a speculative database search against unidentified DNA at the scene run as routine — returns clean, since Burgess's own profile had already been removed from the National DNA Database (2013 mass deletion exercise, per the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012) |
| **9 Jan 2022, AM** | **Nigel Wooley arrested and interviewed — Interview 1** (Appendix B) | Terminated early when Nigel requests a solicitor, after being confronted with CCTV footage (initially denied, then the coat found in his flat makes denial untenable) |
| **9 Jan 2022, PM** | **Nigel Wooley interviewed — Interview 2**, with solicitor R. Okafor present | Admits the restraining-order breach and his real motive (hearing Susan had a new partner); denies any involvement in her death; released on police bail pending further enquiries |
| **11 Jan 2022** | **Burgess canvassed** (Appendix D) — routine house-to-house/trade enquiry, logged by PC Okonkwo, not a suspect interview | Confirms the documented neighbour job, denies any reason to enter number 16, expresses shock at the news; no follow-up |
| — | **Gap: the homeowner at Burgess's next appointment never interviewed** | Would have described him as sweating, short-tempered, hiding his toolbox — nobody asked |
| Ongoing | Investigation focus remains on Nigel Wooley | Strongest available suspect on paper (motive, history, breach, DNA); never charged for lack of conclusive evidence |

| Date | Action | Outcome |
|---|---|---|

| — | **Gap: Haddad's genuine reason for being in the area (buying cannabis at a cuckooed flat, and what he noticed there) never surfaces** | Protected by his own silence; the split-knuckle/tension detail he could have offered is never obtained |

## Cross-case: the near-misses worth being explicit about

- **DS Hannah Ferris personally handled both the Mason interview (2019) and the Wooley investigation (2022)** — three years apart, no reason at the time to connect them, but the same officer had direct contact with the actual killer twice before ever suspecting a pattern existed.
- **Burgess was formally interviewed once (Mason, 2019) and informally canvassed once (Wooley, 2022)** — both on record, never cross-referenced against each other until Ferris's 2025 memo, and even then only as a vague, unnamed recollection.
- **Two separate, genuine witnesses to Burgess's behaviour were never interviewed at all**: the homeowner at his next appointment after killing Wooley, and — in a different case entirely — Haddad, who could have described the operation's tension the night Porterhouse died, had anyone thought to ask him as a witness rather than treat him as a suspect.
- **The one piece of physical evidence that could have closed Mason's case in 2019 — the spanner — was never looked for**, because nobody asked the one procedural question (which tool was used) that would have led to it.
