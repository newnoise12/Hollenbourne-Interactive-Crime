# Hollenbourne — Procedural Documents

*Policy/decision logs, family liaison contact logs, and house-to-house canvass summaries for each case. Same category as the forensic reports and primary interviews — genuine baseline case-file material, available to the whole team from the start of that case's relevant week, not gated behind costed actions. New investigative work students choose to pursue themselves (re-interviews, forensic comparisons, CCTV requests) is what's gated; what the original investigation actually did is not.*

## Sara Butt — missing person report (23 May 2025)

**HOLLENBOURNE POLICE**
**Missing Person Report and Risk Assessment**

Report taken by: PC R. Ahmed
Date/time report received: 23 May 2025, 08:15
Reported by: [Sara Butt's parents], next of kin

---

**Missing person details**

Name: Sara Butt
Age: 24
Last seen: 22 May 2025, approximately 10:49pm, alighting a bus service toward Boresfield
Description: [standard physical description withheld from this exhibit]
Clothing last known: red shirt, dark jeans

**Circumstances**

Sara was returning home from seeing friends, a journey she had made many times before without incident. Her last contact was a WhatsApp message to her parents at 10:49pm reading "back in 10" — sent as she got off the bus, consistent with her usual estimate for the walk home. She did not arrive. Her parents raised the alarm the following morning after repeated attempts to reach her went unanswered overnight, initially assuming she had stayed at a friend's house, as she had done occasionally before.

**Risk assessment: HIGH**

Rationale: No history of going missing previously; a reliable, consistent pattern of contact with her parents; last contact indicated imminent return; no known relationship difficulties, financial pressures, or mental health concerns disclosed by family; phone subsequently unreachable, consistent with the device being off, damaged, or out of signal rather than simply unanswered.

**Immediate actions authorised**

- Family Liaison Officer assigned same day (DC N. Osei)
- Search of the last-known route, focusing initially on the immediate vicinity of the bus stop
- Media appeal authorised at force level given anticipated public interest
- Phone data request submitted (see separate exhibit — WhatsApp message extraction)

---

*This report remained active for eight days until the discovery of a body in Hollen Marsh on 30 May 2025 (see Policy File, Sara Butt case). The high-risk classification and the swift, well-resourced initial response are not in question — the case's subsequent institutional failures lie in the scope of the CCTV canvass (Policy File, Decision 5), not in how seriously the disappearance itself was taken.*

## Sara Butt — phone data extraction (2025, case review)

**HOLLENBOURNE POLICE**
**Digital Forensics — Exhibit Report**

Requested by: Case review team
Device: Sara Butt's mobile phone (recovered, see forensic recovery log)
Extraction method: Standard mobile device forensic extraction

---

**Finding of note**

A WhatsApp message sent from the deceased's device to a contact saved as "Mum & Dad" at **22:49:07 on 22 May 2025**, reading: *"back in 10"*. GPS metadata attached to the message places the device within the expected range of the bus stop on the southern edge of Boresfield at the time of sending. No further outgoing activity from the device after this timestamp. The device's own local clock is consistent with network time at the point of extraction, giving no reason to doubt the accuracy of the timestamp.

**Investigative relevance**

Primarily useful for timeline construction rather than direct identification of any suspect: establishes with confidence the point at which Sara was both alive and in the immediate vicinity of the bus stop, and narrows the window in which the attack must have taken place to the minutes immediately following. Cross-references cleanly with the bus operator's own boarding timestamp (8:52pm) and the Paget Street doorbell camera sighting (approximately 9:45pm) already on file — all three independently timestamped records are consistent with a single continuous account of the evening, though none of them were compared against each other until this review.

## Holly Creagan — witness statement, Swayne alibi (case review, 2025)

*Taken as a routine follow-up alongside the wider case review. Holly has not been told the specific date connects to Sara Butt's death, and doesn't ask — her account is genuinely uncomplicated rather than guarded. Deliberately gives neither a confirmation nor a contradiction: real, ordinary memory failure, with a plain, unremarked reason behind it (drug use together tends to blur one evening into the next). Nothing here should read as suspicious in itself — the ambiguity comes from the plain absence of a clean answer, not from any detail worth flagging.*

*(Criminal Justice Act 1967, s.9)*

Statement of: Holly Creagan
Address: Folkestone Road, Boresfield

This statement, consisting of one page, is true to the best of my knowledge and belief.

---

I've known Colin a few years now, since round when he moved onto the estate. We're not close exactly, but he comes round mine some evenings and we'll use together — that's just how we know each other, really, more than being proper friends.

## Khalid Haddad — historical record summary (case review, 2025)

*A real, findable paper trail — not a recollection, not an "old officer's memory" the way Ferris's Burgess unease works. Deliberately the opposite texture: a formal, official record sitting in a database, discoverable by anyone who thinks to pull it. Gated at cost 1 — low friction, since requesting a named suspect's own history is standard practice, not the kind of cross-agency friction a cell site request involves. Available once Haddad is already a person of interest (which he is from Porterhouse onward).*

**HOLLENBOURNE POLICE**
**Historical record summary — subject: Khalid Haddad**
**Requested by: Case review team**

---

**PNC/CRIS summary**

- 2018: Arrested during a police operation targeting a county lines route operating through Hornchurch. Found at a property used to store product, alongside two older men, both of whom had prior drugs convictions.
- Duty solicitor present at first interview raises concern regarding potential exploitation, citing subject's age (18), immigration history (arrived as an unaccompanied asylum claimant, 2015), and absence of any prior record. NRM referral submitted prior to charge.
- Case status: **discontinued**. Section 45 Modern Slavery Act 2015 defence accepted — offence found to be a direct consequence of exploitation. No conviction recorded.

**NRM outcome (Home Office, Single Competent Authority)**

- Reasonable grounds decision: **positive** (potential victim of modern slavery/criminal exploitation)
- Conclusive grounds decision: **positive**, confirmed some months later

---

*Relevance to case review: this is a formal, positive finding by the Home Office's own competent authority — not a character reference, not a plea in mitigation, an official determination that Haddad was a victim of criminal exploitation as a minor-to-young-adult. Anyone reading his file cold, starting from the 2023 arrest and the "no comment" interview, would reasonably read him as someone with a drugs-adjacent record who won't cooperate. This record inverts that reading entirely, and it was sitting in the system the whole time — nobody thought to pull it until this review. The clearest single document in the case for demonstrating how profiling actually functions: not malice, just nobody connecting a record that already said the opposite of what everyone assumed.*
