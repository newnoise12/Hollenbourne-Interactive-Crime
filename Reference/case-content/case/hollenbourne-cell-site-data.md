# Hollenbourne Case — Cell Site Analysis Reports

*Historic cell site data, requested against named individuals for specific dates already established elsewhere in the case. Each report is gated: a team can only request data against someone already named as a suspect or person of interest through other evidence — this isn't a speculative dragnet available from week one. Cell site data shows which mast/sector a device connected to, not a precise GPS location: it corroborates or complicates an account, it doesn't independently prove presence at an exact address. That limitation is deliberate and should be preserved in how these are presented to students.*

*Each report below now has a matching visual exhibit (PNG, in the outputs folder) — a mapped overlay in the same style real cell site analysts actually produce for investigators and court, not just a table of times and cell IDs. One exhibit per report, individual to that suspect, rather than the earlier combined comparison maps.*

## Martin Burgess — 6 January 2022 (Wooley case)

*Visual exhibit: `exhibit-burgess-wooley-cellsite.png`*

**HOLLENBOURNE POLICE**
**Communications Data — Cell Site Analysis**

Subject: Martin Burgess
Period requested: 6 January 2022, 14:00–18:00
Requesting officer: Case review team

**Finding**

The subject's device connects to a cell sector covering [address redacted — the booked job's street] and the adjoining street (Susan Wooley's address falls within the same sector) from 15:14 continuously through to 16:51, at which point it moves to a sector consistent with travel toward [Featherton — his next logged appointment]. No second booking is recorded at any address within this sector for the period after the first job's expected completion.

**Investigative relevance**

A routine boiler service of the type logged typically runs 30–45 minutes. The device remains within the same sector for approximately 95 minutes — around 45–50 minutes longer than the booked job alone would account for, with no second job logged to explain the remainder. This is consistent with, and independently corroborates, the subject's own later account (2025 arrest interview) of an unbooked visit of roughly 35 minutes at an adjoining address that afternoon. The sector itself is not narrow enough to confirm presence at any single address — this finding establishes an unexplained gap in his account, not a location.

*This is the single most valuable new addition to the Wooley case specifically: the only one of Burgess's three killings with no independent movement-evidence chain until now. Mason has the vehicle/traffic-camera route; Butt has the bus/CCTV/doorbell route; Wooley had nothing beyond Ferris's own unease and the (now-filed) homeowner witness. This closes that gap without over-claiming precision.*

## Martin Burgess — 8 October 2019 (Mason case)

*Visual exhibit: `exhibit-burgess-mason-cellsite.png`*

**HOLLENBOURNE POLICE**
**Communications Data — Cell Site Analysis**

Subject: Martin Burgess
Period requested: 8 October 2019, 15:00–22:00
Requesting officer: Case review team

**Finding**

The device enters the Boresfield sector via Chertsey Street at approximately 15:18, consistent with travel to the booked appointment. It connects to the sector covering Geoff Mason's street from 15:31 to 16:20, matching the appointment itself, before moving to a sector consistent with Harrison Road at approximately 16:33 — a different route out of the estate than the one used to enter it. **No cell activity is recorded for the device that evening.**

**Investigative relevance**

The full afternoon picture — arrival, appointment, and a departure route that doesn't retrace the approach — is confirming rather than new on its own, but it establishes a normal, complete pattern of movement for a routine job, which is exactly the baseline the evening gap stands out against. That gap is the more interesting finding: consistent with the established account, he doesn't have his phone with him for the return trip to the marsh, whether by deliberate precaution or simple incidental caution — it isn't established either way, and doesn't need to be. That evening window is corroborated instead by the traffic camera, ANPR, and doorbell canvass evidence already on file, none of which depend on the phone at all. Worth using this deliberately with students: a genuine gap in one evidence type doesn't undermine what the others independently establish — investigations lean on multiple data sources precisely because no single one covers everything, and a team that expected cell data to confirm every other finding needs to learn that lesson somewhere.

## ANPR (Automatic Number Plate Recognition)

*A genuinely different data source from the existing local authority traffic camera evidence, not a duplicate — the traffic camera is a single council-owned CCTV point showing one night; ANPR is the national police-accessible network, queryable by plate across a wider date range once a registration is known. Both actions below build on, rather than replace, the existing Mason movements chain.*

### Martin Burgess — ANPR history, Patricia Burgess's vehicle

**HOLLENBOURNE POLICE**
**ANPR data request — case review**

Vehicle: KN58 TVP, registered to Patricia Burgess
Requested by: Case review team
Period requested: 1 October 2019 – 31 May 2025 (spanning all three relevant dates)

**Finding**

The vehicle is picked up by ANPR cameras on the approach roads to Hollen Marsh at 9:11pm and 9:54pm on 8 October 2019 — independently corroborating the local authority traffic camera sighting already on file (9:14pm/9:52pm), two separate camera networks reaching the same conclusion without ever having been cross-checked against each other until this review. The same plate returns **no ANPR hits** in the vicinity of Susan Wooley's address on 6 January 2022, or near Hollen Marsh or the Boresfield/Critchley area on 22 May 2025.

**Investigative relevance**

The Mason-night result is confirming, not new. The absence of any hit on the other two dates is the genuinely useful finding here, and worth presenting to students as informative rather than a dead end: it's consistent with, and corroborates, Burgess's already-established methods for those two killings — walking from a neighbouring job for Wooley, bus and on foot for Butt — rather than driving. A negative result from a properly targeted search is still evidence; it closes off an alternative theory (that he drove on those nights too) as cleanly as a positive hit confirms one.

### Full ANPR sweep — Hollen Marsh access roads, 8 October 2019, 21:00–22:00

**HOLLENBOURNE POLICE**
**ANPR data request — case review**

Area: all camera-covered approach roads to Hollen Marsh
Requested by: Case review team
Period requested: 8 October 2019, 21:00–22:00

**Finding**

Four vehicles pass through camera coverage in the requested window. One is the dark 4x4 registered to Patricia Burgess (see above). The other three:

- A taxi, later confirmed (via the operator's own job log) to be en route to an unrelated fare in North-Hollenbourne
- A resident's car, registered to an address on the approach road itself — a normal return home, nothing further to it
- A delivery van, confirmed against the driver's own employer records as working a scheduled round that evening, unconnected to any address relevant to this case

**Investigative relevance**

Deliberately included as investigative texture rather than a fourth lead: an unfiltered sweep returns noise as well as signal, and every one of these three would need to be run down and eliminated in a real investigation before anyone could be confident the fourth was the one that mattered. Worth using this to make an explicit point to students about cost — chasing all three unconnected vehicles individually would be expensive in points for zero investigative return, which is exactly the kind of judgement call a real SIO has to make constantly and mostly invisibly.

### Khalid Haddad — ANPR check, Marsh Road, 6 January 2022

**HOLLENBOURNE POLICE**
**ANPR data request — case review**

Vehicle: [registration], registered to Khalid Haddad
Requested by: Case review team
Period requested: 6 January 2022, 17:30–18:30

**Finding**

The vehicle is picked up by an ANPR camera at the northern end of Marsh Road at 18:04, and again heading back onto Chertsey Street at 18:16 — closely matching the timeline already established through his employer's delivery-app data and his own account in interview (Appendix I).

**Investigative relevance**

Two silos, employer data and the police's own camera network, reaching the same conclusion without ever having been checked against each other. A small, honest demonstration that corroboration has value even when it isn't discovery.

### Full ANPR sweep — Featherton, 6 January 2022, 18:10–18:20

**HOLLENBOURNE POLICE**
**ANPR data request — case review**

Area: Featherton, camera-covered roads only
Requested by: Case review team
Period requested: 6 January 2022, 18:10–18:20

**Finding**

Three vehicles pass through camera coverage in the requested window, besides Haddad's own (above):

- A resident's car, registered to a Featherton address, confirmed as a normal return home
- A delivery van from a different, unrelated courier firm, working a separate scheduled round
- One vehicle whose plate returns **no match on the DVLA database** — inconclusive. Could be a data-capture error (a common, mundane cause), a cloned or false plate, or simply a vehicle passing through moments outside the requested window and misattributed. Not resolvable from ANPR data alone.

**Investigative relevance**

The unmatched plate is the interesting result here, and deliberately left unresolved rather than quietly explained away. It's consistent with — though it doesn't prove — the established picture of a low-level, deniable operation that survives specifically by being hard to pin down through ordinary channels; the same reason Kato and Reece themselves stay unidentifiable through any other evidence in this case. Worth resisting the urge to hand students a clean answer here: real organised crime investigation runs into exactly this kind of dead end constantly, and an unmatched plate is a realistic place for the trail to simply stop.
